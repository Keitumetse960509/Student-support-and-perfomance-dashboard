# Student Support & Education Access Analysis

## Research Question

What factors may influence student attendance and access to educational support?

## Dataset

The dataset contains fictional information for 15 students. It includes age, gender, year level, access challenges, support received, and attendance percentage.

## Key Variables

- **Age:** Age of the student.
- **Gender:** Gender recorded for the fictional student.
- **Year Level:** The student's level of study.
- **Access Challenge:** The main challenge affecting access to education.
- **Support Received:** Whether the student received educational support.
- **Attendance Percentage:** The student's attendance percentage.

## Initial Observations

The dataset suggests that students experience different challenges that may affect their educational participation.

The main access challenges represented in the dataset are:

- Transport
- Financial difficulties
- Internet access

The dataset also shows differences in attendance among students who received support and those who did not.

## Sociological Perspective

Access to education is influenced by more than individual motivation. Social and economic factors such as financial circumstances, transportation, and access to technology can affect students' ability to participate consistently in education.

Educational support may help reduce some of these barriers. However, further research would be needed before drawing conclusions about cause and effect.

## Limitations

This is a small fictional dataset created for learning and portfolio purposes. It does not represent real students or a specific educational institution.

Because the dataset is small, the findings cannot be generalised to the wider student population.

## Key Findings

### 1. Overall Attendance

The average attendance across the 15 students in the sample was **78.0%**.

### 2. Attendance and Educational Support

Students who received educational support had an average attendance of **83.8%**.

Students who did not receive educational support had an average attendance of **66.4%**.

This represents a difference of **17.4 percentage points** between the two groups.

The finding suggests an association between receiving support and higher attendance within this sample. However, the data does not establish that support directly causes higher attendance.

### 3. Attendance by Access Challenge

The average attendance for students experiencing different access challenges was:

- **Transport:** 79.8%
- **Financial difficulties:** 73.6%
- **Internet access:** 80.6%

Financial difficulties had the lowest average attendance in this sample, while internet access had the highest.

### 4. Distribution of Access Challenges

Each of the three access challenges appeared five times in the dataset:

- Transport: 5 students
- Financial difficulties: 5 students
- Internet access: 5 students

Therefore, no single access challenge was more common than another in this sample.

## Interpretation

The findings demonstrate how social and educational factors can be examined using data.

The difference in attendance between students who received support and those who did not may indicate that access to educational support is an important area for further investigation.

The lower attendance associated with financial difficulties may also suggest that economic circumstances can create barriers to consistent participation in education.

However, these findings are based on a small fictional dataset and should not be generalised to the wider student population.
